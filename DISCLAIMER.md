## Not legal advice

The WHS Professional skill ("the skill") produces general guidance on Work Health
and Safety / Occupational Health and Safety matters in Australia and New Zealand.
The skill is **not legal advice** and does not establish a solicitor-client,
advisor-client, or any other professional relationship between the user, the
authors, contributors, or Anthropic.

Where the output of the skill touches on:
- statutory obligations under any Act or Regulation
- rights or duties of any person under the Work Health and Safety Act 2011 (or
  state/territory equivalents), the Occupational Health and Safety Act 2004
  (Vic), or the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 (NZ)
- industrial manslaughter, Category 1 / 2 / 3 prosecutions, or officer due
  diligence obligations
- regulator notifications, notices, or inspections
- coronial, civil, or criminal proceedings

…the user should obtain advice from a qualified Australian or New Zealand legal
practitioner with current admission and relevant practice area expertise before
acting on any output from the skill.

## Not a substitute for current regulatory text

WHS and OHS legislation, regulations, codes of practice, Australian and New
Zealand Standards, and Workplace Exposure Standards change frequently. The
skill is calibrated to a snapshot in time and will become progressively less
accurate as that snapshot ages.

Before relying on any specific regulatory claim, citation, or numeric value
produced by the skill, the user must validate against the current authoritative
source for the relevant jurisdiction, including but not limited to:

- **Federal**: <https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au>, <https://www.legislation.gov.au>

## Not a substitute for qualified WHS practitioners

The skill is designed to assist qualified WHS / OHS / EHS practitioners and
managers in producing efficient outputs. It is not a substitute for the
exercise of professional judgement by a person with the relevant training,
experience, and accreditation.

In particular, the skill must not be relied upon as the sole basis for:
- incident investigation findings in matters involving fatality, serious
  injury, or notifiable events
- risk assessments for high-risk construction work, Major Hazard Facilities,
  or high-energy hazards
- response to regulator notices (improvement, prohibition, non-disturbance)
- legal proceedings of any kind
- design of safety-critical engineering controls
- medical surveillance or fitness-for-work determinations

## Limitations of AI-generated content

The skill is implemented in Claude, a large language model. Like all large
language models, Claude can:
- produce confident-sounding but factually incorrect statements
- omit material facts that the user would consider important
- conflate similar but distinct legislative provisions or codes
- invent document references, section numbers, penalty amounts, or case
  citations that do not exist
- apply outdated information from its training data

The user is responsible for verifying the accuracy of every material claim
before acting on it.

## Worked example (fictional)

The worked example in `references/company.md` is **entirely fictional**.
"Meridian Facilities Group (MFG)" does not exist. The example was authored
for this skill as a structural reference showing how a large Australian
organisation might configure its WHS context — the document codes, risk
matrix, severity classes, notification timeframes, named programs, and
governance cadence are all invented for illustration and must not be
treated as any real organisation's settings.

No real organisation's internal documents, procedures, or data are included
in this skill. Any resemblance between the fictional example and a real
organisation's documents, branding, or practices is coincidental.

Users adapting the skill for their own organisation should replace this
content entirely with their own validated context before relying on the
skill operationally.
