## 1. Scope of investigation

Investigation covers the events of 18 April 2026 at the Sydney Distribution
Centre, specifically the forklift movements in Aisle 14 between 14:00 and 14:30
and the related pedestrian stocktake activity. Includes the traffic management
arrangements, operator training records, and the stocktake activity
authorisation route. Other site forklift operations are not in scope except
where comparable findings emerge from this investigation.

## 3. Incident overview

At 14:22 on 18 April 2026, Operator A was reversing a 2.5-tonne counterbalance
forklift down Aisle 14 carrying a pallet to the racking face at location 14-32.
Pedestrian Worker B was conducting a manual bin-location stocktake in the same
aisle. The forklift, travelling at approximately 6 km/h in reverse, came within
1 metre of Pedestrian B before stopping. Pedestrian B had not been detected by
Operator A; Pedestrian B was on the operator's right-rear blind side.

Operator A heard a colleague in the adjacent aisle shout, stopped immediately,
and saw Pedestrian B for the first time. No contact occurred. Both workers
reported the incident within the hour.

## 5. PEEPO summary

- **People**: Operator A — current HRWL (LF class), six years experience at
  this site, no prior incidents. Pedestrian B — current stocktake competency,
  two years experience. Both reported alert and well; no fatigue indicators on
  pre-shift assessment.
- **Environment**: Aisle width 3.2 m (within site design spec); lighting
  adequate; ambient noise level high due to adjacent racking activity, which
  likely masked forklift reverse alarm.
- **Equipment**: Forklift current on inspection; reverse alarm operational
  (constant tone, ~90 dB at 1 m); no reverse camera or proximity warning system fitted.
- **Procedures**: Site Traffic Management Plan requires physical exclusion
  zone for stocktake activity in active aisles; verbal authorisation route
  taken did not trigger physical exclusion zone application.
- **Organisation**: Stocktake activity had been authorised verbally for
  "small spot-checks" without exclusion zone for at least 18 months prior to
  this event, per Shift Supervisor and HSR statements.

## 8. Incident risk assessment

**Actual risk**: Consequence 1 (no injury) × Likelihood Possible = Low
**Potential risk**: Consequence 5 (single fatality) × Likelihood Possible = Critical

**Potential rationale**: A 2.5-tonne forklift travelling in reverse at ~6 km/h
with a pedestrian on the blind side creates a credible worst-case outcome of
fatal crushing or impact. The 1-metre clearance was the result of a
third-party intervention (colleague shout), not the design of the safety
system.

## 10. Conclusions

This was a HiPo near-miss; the absence of contact reflects intervention by a
third party, not the design of the safety system. The proximate cause —
forklift reversing without 360° check — is a foreseeable individual error in a
system where pedestrian access to active forklift aisles is routinely
permitted without physical separation. The organisational driver is gradual
drift from the Traffic Management Plan over an 18-month period, normalised
because "nothing had happened."

The corrective actions address the system condition, not the individual
operators. The network-wide review of TMP application is the most important
systemic action; the pattern of TMP drift is unlikely to be isolated to this
site.
