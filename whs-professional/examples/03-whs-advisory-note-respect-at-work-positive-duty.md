# WHS Advisory Note — Discharge of SDA s 47C Positive Duty

**To**: Chief People Officer
**From**: A. Singh, Head of WHS
**Date**: 27 May 2026
**Subject**: SDA s 47C positive duty — current state and gap analysis against AHRC seven standards
**Ref**: AN-2026-009
**Classification**: Internal

---

## 1. Issue

The Sex Discrimination Act 1984 (Cth) s 47C positive duty, inserted by the
Anti-Discrimination and Human Rights Legislation Amendment (Respect at Work)
Act 2022 (Cth), has been in force since 13 December 2022 (assent 12 December
2022). AHRC compliance and enforcement powers commenced 12 December 2023. We
have completed substantial WHS work on psychosocial hazards under the model
WHS Regulations rr 55A–55D (in NSW, Part 3.2 Division 11), but we have not
yet mapped that work explicitly to the AHRC's seven standards for compliance
with the positive duty.
This advisory recommends the bridging work required to discharge the SDA duty
and to be able to demonstrate it.

## 2. Legislative basis

- **Sex Discrimination Act 1984 (Cth) s 47C** — positive duty on an employer
  or a person conducting a business or undertaking to take reasonable and
  proportionate measures to eliminate, as far as possible, sex
  discrimination, sexual harassment, sex-based harassment, conduct creating a
  hostile workplace environment on the ground of sex, and related victimisation
- **AHRC enforcement powers** (from 12 December 2023) — inquiry into
  compliance, compliance notices, enforceable undertakings, application to
  Federal Court
- **Model WHS Regulations rr 55A–55D** (in NSW, WHS Regulation Part 3.2
  Division 11) — psychosocial risk management duty; covers the same conduct
  from a different statutory angle
- **AHRC Guidelines on the positive duty** (August 2023) — seven standards
  for compliance: leadership, culture, knowledge, risk management, support,
  reporting and response, and monitoring, evaluation and transparency

## 3. Risk assessment

If the AHRC commences an inquiry into our compliance with s 47C, the
evidentiary expectation is documented action against each of the seven
standards. Our current psychosocial risk register and EAP arrangements address
the WHS duty but do not explicitly map to the AHRC framework.

Likelihood of inquiry: low–moderate (no current public complaints; sector
profile attracts periodic AHRC attention; recent industry inquiries have
covered comparable organisations).
Consequence if inquiry finds non-compliance: compliance notice, potential
enforceable undertaking, reputational impact, ongoing regulatory engagement
burden. Estimated risk under organisational matrix: B.

## 4. Current state

| AHRC Standard | Current state | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Leadership | CEO has signed the WHS policy statement | No specific s 47C accountability statement; no Board-level visibility |
| 2. Culture | Cultural survey conducted Q3 2025; no specific sexual harassment prevalence metric | Sexual harassment prevalence data not collected |
| 3. Knowledge | All-staff harassment awareness training; manager-level training | Training not mapped to s 47C; no refresh schedule beyond 2-yearly |
| 4. Risk management | Psychosocial hazard register includes sexual harassment as a category | Risk register not reviewed since Dec 2024; controls not specifically rated |
| 5. Support | EAP available; HR pathway documented | No trauma-informed support training for first responders to disclosures |
| 6. Reporting and response | One reporting pathway (HR); investigation procedure documented | Multiple safe pathways recommended; investigator competency not externally validated |
| 7. Monitoring, evaluation and transparency | Annual harassment complaints reported to ELT | No external transparency reporting; effectiveness measures not defined |

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Allocate leadership accountability**: assign Executive Sponsor for s 47C
   compliance (recommended: CPO), with reporting line to CEO and Board. Document
   in Charter. *Owner*: Company Secretary. *Due*: 30 June 2026.

2. **Map controls to seven standards**: produce a single-page compliance
   evidence map, refreshed annually. Use as input to Board reporting and as the
   primary document served in any AHRC inquiry response. *Owner*: WHS + People
   jointly. *Due*: 31 July 2026.

3. **Targeted training refresh**: training for managers and people leaders
   rebuilt to specifically address the seven standards, including bystander
   intervention. Annual refresh thereafter. *Owner*: L&D. *Due*: 30 September
   2026.

4. **Multiple reporting pathways**: introduce confidential external reporting
   line (third-party provider) in addition to the current internal HR pathway.
   *Owner*: People. *Due*: 30 September 2026.

5. **Investigator competency**: external accreditation of nominated internal
   investigators (Workplace Behaviour Investigations certification or
   equivalent). *Owner*: People. *Due*: 31 December 2026.

6. **Transparency reporting**: include sexual harassment prevalence and
   response metrics in Annual Report; align to AHRC's recommended disclosure
   framework. *Owner*: Company Secretary + Communications. *Due*: FY26 Annual
   Report cycle.

## 6. Next steps

ELT decision required on Recommendations 1 and 6 (governance and disclosure
scope). Other recommendations can proceed under operational delegation. Suggest
brief paper to ELT June meeting and full implementation program to Board
October meeting.
