# Safety Alert — Electrical Contact during Switchboard Cleaning

| **Function**: Property Services / Building Maintenance | **Alert ref**: SA-2026-014 |

---

## What happened?

During routine quarterly cleaning of a main distribution switchboard at a tenanted
commercial site in Parramatta NSW, a maintenance worker made contact with an
unisolated 415V bus bar while attempting to remove dust from the rear of the panel.
The worker felt the contact but did not receive a sustained shock — the contact
area was small and the worker was wearing rated electrical gloves rated for the
voltage.

Initial inquiry confirmed:
- The switchboard had been visually inspected but not electrically isolated before
  cleaning commenced
- The work order described the task as "general cleaning" without referencing
  electrical isolation requirements
- The worker held a general electrical induction but no switchboard-specific
  competency assessment

**Critical risk**: Electrical Safety
**Critical control absent**: Verified isolation before access to live components

*[Image: switchboard panel showing dust accumulation and bus bar exposed; arrow indicating point of contact. Caption: rear of MSB-3 prior to isolation — bus bar at top of frame is unisolated 415V three-phase.]*

---

## What did we learn?

- Cleaning is electrical work where it brings the worker within arm's reach of
  live components — it must be planned as electrical work, not as cleaning work
- "Routine" tasks degrade isolation discipline; tasks treated as low-risk
  receive proportionally less control attention over time
- Contractor competency at this site was not specifically assessed for
  switchboard work; the work order did not specify isolation requirements,
  and the contractor proceeded on the basis of the documented scope

---

## Required action

- All switchboard cleaning work orders updated by 14 June 2026 to require formal
  isolation procedure (LOTO) and electrical worker competent for switchboard
  work present
- Site-specific switchboard inventory reviewed by Property Services within 30
  days; all panels classified by access requirements
- Contractor scope variations issued to all FM cleaning contractors to specify
  electrical isolation requirements; cleaning of energised switchboards is
  prohibited

---

## Discussion questions

- How could this happen on your site? Are routine maintenance tasks involving
  electrical equipment isolated before work begins?
- What can you do to prevent it happening again? When you see "cleaning" on a
  work order for an electrical room or panel, do you know if the area is
  electrically live, and is the right competency on site?
