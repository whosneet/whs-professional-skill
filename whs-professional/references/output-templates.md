## 1. Safety Alert

### Purpose
A safety alert communicates a recent incident or hazard to workers and leadership
to prevent recurrence. One page. Visual-first. Learning-focused.

### When to use
- After any HiPo, LTI, or fatality — issue within 24–72 hours
- After a dangerous incident or near miss with broad applicability
- To share a lesson from another site, industry, or jurisdiction

### Tone
Direct. No blame. Learning-focused. Short sentences. Frontline accessible.
Avoid: passive voice, corporate language, safety clichés.

### Template Structure

**HEADER ROW** (two-column)
| Function: [Business function / division] | Alert Title: [Short, specific title] |
|---|---|

---
**WHAT HAPPENED?** (left column, with image placeholder on right)
- A simple explanation of what occurred — 2–4 sentences, factual, no names, no blame
- For alerts involving a critical risk: include reference to the critical risk and
  which controls were absent or failed
- Bullet points for key impacts (3 max)
- Image on the right with caption: descriptive, conveys what the photo is showing

---
**WHAT DID WE LEARN?**
Explain the lessons so the reader fully understands the impact.
- Main lesson 1
- Main lesson 2
- Main lesson 3

---
**REQUIRED ACTION** *(Delete this section if not applicable)*
- [Specific action — present tense, direct]
- [Specific action]

---
**DISCUSSION QUESTIONS** *(Discuss at next pre-start or team meeting)*
- How could this happen on your job?
- What can you do to prevent it happening again?

---
**FOOTER ROW** (four-column)
| Further Information | Authorised By | [blank] | Useful Resources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Name, Role, Phone, Email | Name, Role, Phone, Email | | Link to Document |

### Drafting Notes
- Keep total body word count under 250 words — this is a one-page document
- Image is essential — use incident diagram, site photo, or hazard image
- "Required Action" section — delete if actions are already captured in the incident management system corrective
  actions and distributed separately; include if immediate field actions are needed
- Discussion questions are fixed in the template — customise only where genuinely
  relevant to the specific hazard
- For alerts involving a critical risk: explicitly name the critical risk category and
  state which critical control failed or was absent — this is mandatory for HiPo and
  high-severity incidents

## 2. Toolbox Talk

### Purpose
A 10–15 minute facilitated safety discussion for a work team.
Conversational, not a lecture. Designed to generate discussion, not just deliver information.

### When to use
- Routine scheduled toolbox sessions (weekly, fortnightly)
- Following a safety alert or incident — reinforce the lesson locally
- When introducing a new hazard, procedure, or work task
- Pre-task briefing for high-risk activities

### Tone
Conversational. Plain English. Short paragraphs — these are read aloud.
Include discussion questions to prompt worker engagement. Workers should talk more than
the facilitator.

### Template

---
**TOOLBOX TALK**
**Topic**: [Topic title]
**Duration**: 10–15 minutes
**Audience**: [Work group / role]
**Date**: [DD Month YYYY]
**Facilitator**: ___________________

---
**WHY ARE WE TALKING ABOUT THIS?**
[1–3 sentences. The reason this topic is relevant right now — recent incident,
upcoming task, seasonal hazard, regulatory change. Make it real and local.]

---
**WHAT'S THE HAZARD?**
[Describe the hazard clearly. What can go wrong. What the consequences are.
Use real examples from the industry or this site where possible.]

---
**WHAT ARE THE CONTROLS?**
[List the controls in place. Reference hierarchy of controls where relevant.
Explain why the controls work, not just what they are.]
- [Control 1]
- [Control 2]
- [Control 3]

---
**DISCUSSION QUESTIONS** *(Ask these — let the team answer)*
1. [Question 1 — open-ended, relevant to the hazard]
   → What to listen for: [Key points the facilitator should draw out]
2. [Question 2]
   → What to listen for: [Key points]
3. [Question 3]
   → What to listen for: [Key points]

---
**KEY MESSAGES** *(summarise at the end)*
- [Message 1 — one sentence]
- [Message 2 — one sentence]
- [Message 3 — one sentence]

---
**ACTIONS FROM TODAY'S TOOLBOX**
| Action | Owner | By when |
|---|---|---|
| | | |

**Attendance**: *(attach sign-on sheet or capture in system)*

---

### Drafting Notes
- Total reading length: 400–600 words (facilitators expand in delivery)
- Each discussion question should have a clear purpose — don't pad with extras
- "What to listen for" helps inexperienced facilitators draw out correct responses
- Avoid yes/no questions — "Is everyone wearing PPE?" is not a discussion question
- Excellent toolbox talks reference a real recent event (local or industry) at the start

## 3. WHS Advisory Note

### Purpose
A structured written advisory to management, a PCBU, or a client on a WHS matter.
Used for: regulatory advice, risk treatment recommendations, compliance gaps,
incident prevention recommendations, program guidance.

### Format
Professional memo. Structured sections. Referenced. Action-oriented conclusion.

### Template

---
**WHS ADVISORY NOTE**

**To**: [Name / Role / Organisation]
**From**: [Advisor name / Role]
**Date**: [DD Month YYYY]
**Subject**: [Topic — specific, not generic]
**Ref**: [Advisory reference number if applicable]
**Classification**: [Internal / Confidential / Privileged]

---
**1. ISSUE**
[2–4 sentences. What is the issue, hazard, or compliance matter being addressed.
Why is this advisory being prepared now.]

**2. LEGISLATIVE / STANDARD BASIS**
[Cite the relevant obligations. Reference specific sections. Quote the duty precisely
if relevant. For AU: cite the Act + section + jurisdiction. For NZ: cite HSWA section.]

**3. RISK ASSESSMENT**
[Current risk level (likelihood × consequence). What harm could result if not addressed.
Estimate exposure — who, how many, how often.]

**4. CURRENT STATE**
[What is currently in place. Where the gap or deficiency is. Be specific.]

**5. RECOMMENDATION**
[Specific recommendations — numbered list. What should be done, by whom, by when.
Apply hierarchy of controls. Reference relevant codes of practice, standards, or guidance.]

1. [Recommendation 1]
2. [Recommendation 2]
3. [Recommendation 3]

**6. NEXT STEPS**
[Immediate actions required. Who is responsible. Timeline for response to this advisory.]

---
*This advisory is based on information available at the date of preparation.
If circumstances change, this advice should be reviewed.*

## 5. Alert vs Bulletin — When to Use Which

Alerts are typically **mandatory for notifiable incidents** under most organisations' procedures, unless deemed unnecessary by the relevant business manager or WHS leader.

Use an **Alert** when: immediate implementation is required prior to the next shift commencing; or there is significant impact on business operations. Displayed on noticeboards and the intranet.

Use a **Bulletin** when: the change can be implemented at the most logical opportunity; or the change is beneficial but does not address a current risk or exposure.

When alerts or bulletins are shared across business groups: write clearly for the broader audience; define all acronyms and BU-specific language; route through the central Group WHS team for publishing.

For investigations subject to legal privilege: contact the Group WHS team before sharing lessons learnt — sharing privileged information may result in loss of privilege.

## 8. WHS Policy Template

### Purpose
High-level commitment statement. Sets intent and assigns accountability.
Not a procedure — no step-by-step instructions.

### Template

---

**Document no.**: [Ref] | **Version**: [n.n] | **Effective date**: [DD/MM/YYYY]
**Review date**: [DD/MM/YYYY] | **Owner**: [Role]

---
**COMMITMENT**
[Organisation name] is committed to providing and maintaining a safe and healthy workplace
for all workers and others affected by our work. We recognise that the effective management
of work health and safety is both a legal obligation and a core business value.

**SCOPE**
This policy applies to [describe scope: all operations, specific divisions, AU/NZ, etc.],
including all employees, contractors, subcontractors, labour hire workers, and visitors.

**OBLIGATIONS**
[Organisation] will, so far as is reasonably practicable:
- Eliminate or minimise risks to the health, safety, and welfare of workers
- Consult with workers on matters that affect their health and safety
- Comply with all relevant WHS legislation and applicable codes of practice
- Provide information, instruction, training, and supervision to enable safe work
- Investigate incidents and implement corrective actions to prevent recurrence
- Continuously improve WHS performance through monitoring, review, and reporting

**ACCOUNTABILITY**
- The Board / CEO: [accountability statement]
- Managers and Supervisors: [accountability statement]
- Workers: [accountability statement]
- WHS Team: [accountability statement]

**REVIEW**
This policy will be reviewed [annually / every 2 years] or following significant changes
to the business, legislation, or following a serious incident.

**Signed**: _____________________ [CEO / MD]  **Date**: ___________

## 9. WHS Procedure Template

### Purpose
Detailed instructions for performing a specific WHS activity consistently and safely.
More detailed than policy; less task-specific than a SWMS.

### Template Structure

**Document title**: [e.g., Incident Investigation Procedure]
**Document no.**: [Ref] | **Version**: [n.n] | **Effective date**: [DD/MM/YYYY]
**Review date**: [DD/MM/YYYY] | **Owner**: [Role] | **Authorised by**: [Role]

**1. Purpose** — Why this procedure exists
**2. Scope** — What and who it applies to
**3. Definitions** — Key terms used in the procedure
**4. Responsibilities** — Who does what (table format: Role | Responsibility)
**5. Procedure** — Numbered steps; clear, instructional language; cross-references to forms
**6. Related documents** — Other policies, procedures, forms, legislation referenced
**7. Review history** — Version, date, change summary

## 11. Risk Register

### Purpose
A live register of identified risks at the organisation, business unit, project,
or contract level. Distinct from the incident system (which captures events) —
the risk register captures *potential* events and the controls in place.

### When to use
- Organisational enterprise risk register (typically annual review with quarterly updates)
- Project risk register (live; updated at milestone reviews)
- Critical risk register (separate, focused on fatal/catastrophic risk — cross-ref `frameworks.md` §7)
- BU or contract risk register (cascade from enterprise level)

### Structure

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Risk ID | Unique reference (e.g., R-2026-014) |
| Risk title | Short descriptor (e.g., "Forklift / pedestrian interface") |
| Risk category | WHS, Environment, Compliance, Financial, Reputational, Strategic, Operational |
| Risk description | What can go wrong, how, and the consequence path |
| Threats / causes | What can initiate the risk (cross-ref bowtie if used) |
| Inherent risk | Likelihood × Consequence before controls (per organisation matrix — see `company.md`) |
| Existing controls | What's in place (engineering, administrative, PPE, monitoring) |
| Control effectiveness | e.g. Adequate / Partially Adequate / Inadequate (use your organisation's rating scale — see `company.md`) |
| Residual risk | Likelihood × Consequence after controls |
| Risk owner | Named accountable person (not role) |
| Treatment plan | Further action to reduce; owner; due date |
| Target risk | Risk level after treatment plan implemented |
| Monitoring | Verification cadence; KPI; CCV link if critical risk |
| Last reviewed | Date; reviewer |
| Next review | Date; trigger conditions for earlier review |

### Population guidance
- Aim for 30–80 risks at enterprise level (more = unworkable; fewer = under-identified)
- Each risk should map to either a critical risk taxonomy (`company.md` §8) or a
  defined enterprise risk category
- Risks at the highest residual ratings (e.g. Critical or High) require senior
  leader acceptance (per `company.md` risk matrix)
- Treatment plans must be specific, assigned, time-bound

### Common errors
- Risk register and incident register conflated
- Risks described in passive voice without clear consequence
- Control effectiveness assessed by the control owner (inherent conflict)
- "Risk register exists" treated as the control rather than the risks being actively managed

## 12. Bowtie Diagram Template

### Purpose
Visual representation of a critical risk showing threats, top event, consequences,
preventive controls, and recovery controls in a single diagram. Used for critical
risk management, board reporting, and pre-task planning for high-energy work.

### When to use
- One per critical risk in the organisation's critical risk taxonomy
- Pre-task for high-energy non-routine work
- Post-incident analysis (which controls failed?)
- Board reporting (visual representation of critical risk control posture)

### Structure

```
[ THREATS ]   [ PREVENTION ]   [ TOP EVENT ]   [ MITIGATION ]   [ CONSEQUENCES ]
    |              |                 |                |                |
  Threat 1  →  Barrier 1.1  →                   →  Barrier 2.1  →  Outcome A
              Barrier 1.2                          Barrier 2.2     Outcome B
              Barrier 1.3                          Barrier 2.3
  Threat 2  →  Barrier 1.4  →     TOP EVENT  →   Barrier 2.4  →
              Barrier 1.5         (hazard          Barrier 2.5
              Barrier 1.6        release)
  Threat 3  →  Barrier 1.7  →                  →   Barrier 2.6  →
              Barrier 1.8                          Barrier 2.7
```

### Required fields per element

**Top event**: defined point at which the hazard is released
**Threats** (3-6 typical): plausible ways the top event could occur
**Prevention barriers** (per threat, 2-5 typical): controls preventing the threat from reaching the top event
**Consequences** (2-5 typical): outcomes if the top event occurs
**Mitigation barriers** (per consequence, 2-5 typical): controls reducing severity
**Degradation factors**: things that can weaken each barrier
**Degradation controls**: controls on the degradation factors

### Per-barrier metadata

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Barrier description | What the control is |
| Type | Engineering / Administrative / PPE |
| Owner | Accountable role |
| Verification method | How the barrier is confirmed in place (inspection, CCV, audit) |
| Verification frequency | Daily / weekly / monthly per criticality |
| Last verified | Date |
| Status | In place / Degraded / Absent |

> Worked example: see `investigation.md` §13 for a fully populated bowtie for
> "fall from height during rooftop maintenance".

## 13. WHS Strategy Document

### Purpose
A 3-year (typical) strategic statement of WHS direction, ambition, and capability
investment. Distinct from the WHS Annual Plan (which is the 12-month operational
roll-out of the strategy).

### Tone
Strategic, not operational. The strategy answers "what does good look like in
3 years and how do we get there?" — not "what are we doing this quarter?"

### Structure

**1. Executive summary** (1 page)
Position; key strategic priorities; investment required; expected outcomes.

**2. Context**
Organisational strategy alignment; regulatory environment; risk profile;
where the WHS function is starting from.

**3. Strategic objectives** (3-5)
For each: the outcome, the rationale (why this objective), how progress
will be measured (beyond TRIFR), the dependencies.

**4. Capability uplift required**
What the function needs to deliver the strategy: people, technology,
processes, governance.

**5. Investment case**
Resource implications (function, technology, intervention budgets); ROI
narrative; risk of not investing.

**6. Governance**
How strategy is monitored; cadence of strategy review; refresh triggers.

**7. Year 1 priorities and milestones**
What's in the first 12 months (the bridge to the WHS Annual Plan §15
below).

### Length
12-25 pages typical. Longer is usually worse — strategy should be
communicable.

> Strategy frameworks and structuring guidance: cross-ref `strategy-function.md`
> §1.

## 14. WHS Function Org Chart and RACI

### Purpose
Visual representation of the WHS function structure plus a RACI matrix that
defines responsibility, accountability, consultation, and information rights
for typical WHS work.

### Purpose
Quarterly (minimum) briefing pack for officers — board, executive, senior

## 23. Regulator Notification Script

### Purpose
Scripts the first phone call to the WHS regulator after a notifiable incident.
The model WHS Act s 38 requires the PCBU to notify the regulator **immediately
after becoming aware** of a notifiable incident, by the fastest possible means
— in practice, a phone call. The call is the start of the regulatory record:
be prepared, factual, and complete.

> Notifiable incident criteria and jurisdictional regulator contacts:
> cross-ref `legislation.md` §5.

### Preparation checklist (minutes, not hours — do not delay the call)
- [ ] Confirm the incident is notifiable — death, serious injury or illness, or
      dangerous incident (model WHS Act ss 35–37) — check against `legislation.md`
- [ ] Confirm who calls — the authorised person under your organisation's
      procedure (see `company.md`); typically the site/contract manager or the
      duty WHS manager
- [ ] Confirm scene preservation is underway (s 39) — site secured; nothing
      disturbed except to assist an injured person, make the site safe, or as
      directed by police or an inspector
- [ ] Assemble the known facts: time, location, people involved, injuries,
      current care, immediate actions taken
- [ ] Trigger internal notifications in parallel per `company.md` — the
      regulator call does not replace the internal chain

### Phone script structure

**1. Caller identification**
"My name is [name]. I am the [role] at [PCBU legal name]. My direct contact
number is [number]."

**2. PCBU details**
- PCBU legal name and ABN
- Site address where the incident occurred (and site contact if different)

**3. What happened — facts only**
- 2–4 sentences. Known facts only. No speculation about cause. No admissions.
- "At approximately [time], [what occurred, in plain factual terms]."

**4. When and where**
- Date and time of the incident; specific location within the site
- When the PCBU became aware (if different)

**5. Injured person status and care**
- Name, age, role (worker / contractor / member of the public)
- Nature of injuries as currently known
- Current care: first aid on site / ambulance / hospital (which one)

**6. Immediate actions taken**
- Care for the injured person; making the area safe; stopping the task;
  isolating plant; notifying emergency services

**7. Scene preservation status**
- Confirm the site is secured and undisturbed per s 39; note anything moved to
  assist the injured person or make the area safe

**8. Close the call**
- "Can I please have the notification reference number?"
- Ask whether written notification is required, in what form, and by when
- Record any directions given (e.g. non-disturbance, inspector attending)

### After the call
- If directed, provide written notification within 48 hours in the approved
  form (model WHS Act s 38)
- Record the call: time, regulator officer's name, reference number, directions
- Retain a record of the notification for at least 5 years (s 38)
- Maintain scene preservation until an inspector releases the site or directs
  otherwise (s 39)

### NZ variant
Under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, notify **WorkSafe New Zealand**
as soon as possible after becoming aware of a notifiable event (HSWA s 56),
and preserve the site until authorised (HSWA s 55). The same script structure
applies; quote the NZBN rather than an ABN.

### Cautions
- Stick to known facts. Do not speculate on cause, fault, or what "should
  have" happened — classification and causation come later
- Do not make admissions of liability — the call is a notification, not a
  statement
- If the regulator asks for investigation material or detailed records during
  the call, take the request on notice and route it through the escalation
  path — legal professional privilege questions go to legal counsel first
  (see `company.md`)
- Note the exact time of the call and everything said — the notifier's
  contemporaneous record may be the only one
- Never delay notification to "get the story straight" — notify immediately
  with what is known and supplement later
