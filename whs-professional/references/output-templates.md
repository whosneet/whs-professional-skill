## 1. Safety Alert

### Purpose
A safety alert communicates a recent incident or hazard to workers and leadership
to prevent recurrence. One page. Visual-first. Learning-focused.

### When to use
- After any HiPo, LTI, or fatality — issue within 24–72 hours
- After a dangerous incident or near miss with broad applicability
- To share a lesson from another site, industry, or jurisdiction

### Tone
Direct. No blame. Learning-focused. Short sentences. Frontline accessible.
Avoid: passive voice, corporate language, safety clichés.

### Template Structure

**HEADER ROW** (two-column)
| Function: [Business function / division] | Alert Title: [Short, specific title] |

---
**WHAT HAPPENED?** (left column, with image placeholder on right)
- A simple explanation of what occurred — 2–4 sentences, factual, no names, no blame
- For Zero Harm alerts: include reference to the critical risk and which controls were
  absent or failed
- Bullet points for key impacts (3 max)
- Image on the right with caption: descriptive, conveys what the photo is showing

---
**WHAT DID WE LEARN?**
Explain the lessons so the reader fully understands the impact.
- Main lesson 1
- Main lesson 2
- Main lesson 3

---
**REQUIRED ACTION** *(Delete this section if not applicable)*
- [Specific action — present tense, direct]
- [Specific action]

---
**DISCUSSION QUESTIONS** *(Discuss at next pre-start or team meeting)*
- How could this happen on your job?
- What can you do to prevent it happening again?

---
**FOOTER ROW** (four-column)
| Further Information | Authorised By | [blank] | Useful Resources |
| Name, Role, Phone, Email | Name, Role, Phone, Email | | Link to Document |

### Drafting Notes
- Keep total body word count under 250 words — this is a one-page document
- Image is essential — use incident diagram, site photo, or hazard image
- "Required Action" section — delete if actions are already captured in the incident management system corrective
  actions and distributed separately; include if immediate field actions are needed
- Discussion questions are fixed in the template — customise only where genuinely
  relevant to the specific hazard
- For Zero Harm alerts: explicitly name the critical risk category and state which
  critical control failed or was absent — this is mandatory for HiPo and severity 4–6

## 2. Toolbox Talk

### Purpose
A 10–15 minute facilitated safety discussion for a work team.
Conversational, not a lecture. Designed to generate discussion, not just deliver information.

### When to use
- Routine scheduled toolbox sessions (weekly, fortnightly)
- Following a safety alert or incident — reinforce the lesson locally
- When introducing a new hazard, procedure, or work task
- Pre-task briefing for high-risk activities

### Tone
Conversational. Plain English. Short paragraphs — these are read aloud.
Include discussion questions to prompt worker engagement. Workers should talk more than
the facilitator.

### Template

---
**TOOLBOX TALK**
**Topic**: [Topic title]
**Duration**: 10–15 minutes
**Audience**: [Work group / role]
**Date**: [DD Month YYYY]
**Facilitator**: ___________________

---
**WHY ARE WE TALKING ABOUT THIS?**
[1–3 sentences. The reason this topic is relevant right now — recent incident,
upcoming task, seasonal hazard, regulatory change. Make it real and local.]

---
**WHAT'S THE HAZARD?**
[Describe the hazard clearly. What can go wrong. What the consequences are.
Use real examples from the industry or this site where possible.]

---
**WHAT ARE THE CONTROLS?**
[List the controls in place. Reference hierarchy of controls where relevant.
Explain why the controls work, not just what they are.]
- [Control 1]
- [Control 2]
- [Control 3]

---
**DISCUSSION QUESTIONS** *(Ask these — let the team answer)*
1. [Question 1 — open-ended, relevant to the hazard]
   → What to listen for: [Key points the facilitator should draw out]
2. [Question 2]
   → What to listen for: [Key points]
3. [Question 3]
   → What to listen for: [Key points]

---
**KEY MESSAGES** *(summarise at the end)*
- [Message 1 — one sentence]
- [Message 2 — one sentence]
- [Message 3 — one sentence]

---
**ACTIONS FROM TODAY'S TOOLBOX**
| Action | Owner | By when |
|---|---|---|
| | | |

**Attendance**: *(attach sign-on sheet or capture in system)*

---

### Drafting Notes
- Total reading length: 400–600 words (facilitators expand in delivery)
- Each discussion question should have a clear purpose — don't pad with extras
- "What to listen for" helps inexperienced facilitators draw out correct responses
- Avoid yes/no questions — "Is everyone wearing PPE?" is not a discussion question
- Excellent toolbox talks reference a real recent event (local or industry) at the start

## 3. WHS Advisory Note

### Purpose
A structured written advisory to management, a PCBU, or a client on a WHS matter.
Used for: regulatory advice, risk treatment recommendations, compliance gaps,
incident prevention recommendations, program guidance.

### Format
Professional memo. Structured sections. Referenced. Action-oriented conclusion.

### Template

---
**WHS ADVISORY NOTE**

**To**: [Name / Role / Organisation]
**From**: [Advisor name / Role]
**Date**: [DD Month YYYY]
**Subject**: [Topic — specific, not generic]
**Ref**: [Advisory reference number if applicable]
**Classification**: [Internal / Confidential / Privileged]

---
**1. ISSUE**
[2–4 sentences. What is the issue, hazard, or compliance matter being addressed.
Why is this advisory being prepared now.]

**2. LEGISLATIVE / STANDARD BASIS**
[Cite the relevant obligations. Reference specific sections. Quote the duty precisely
if relevant. For AU: cite the Act + section + jurisdiction. For NZ: cite HSWA section.]

**3. RISK ASSESSMENT**
[Current risk level (likelihood × consequence). What harm could result if not addressed.
Estimate exposure — who, how many, how often.]

**4. CURRENT STATE**
[What is currently in place. Where the gap or deficiency is. Be specific.]

**5. RECOMMENDATION**
[Specific recommendations — numbered list. What should be done, by whom, by when.
Apply hierarchy of controls. Reference relevant codes of practice, standards, or guidance.]

1. [Recommendation 1]
2. [Recommendation 2]
3. [Recommendation 3]

**6. NEXT STEPS**
[Immediate actions required. Who is responsible. Timeline for response to this advisory.]

---
*This advisory is based on information available at the date of preparation.
If circumstances change, this advice should be reviewed.*

## 4. Preliminary Internal Incident Notification (PIIN)

## 5. Alert vs Bulletin — When to Use Which

Alerts are typically **mandatory for notifiable incidents** under most organisations' procedures, unless deemed unnecessary by the relevant business manager or Zero Harm leader.

Use an **Alert** when: immediate implementation is required prior to the next shift commencing; or there is significant impact on business operations. Displayed on noticeboards and the intranet.

Use a **Bulletin** when: the change can be implemented at the most logical opportunity; or the change is beneficial but does not address a current risk or exposure.

When alerts or bulletins are shared across business groups: write clearly for the broader audience; define all acronyms and BU-specific language; route through the central Zero Harm function for publishing.

For investigations subject to legal privilege: contact Group Zero Harm before sharing lessons learnt — sharing privileged information may result in loss of privilege.

## 6. WHS Policy Template

### Purpose
High-level commitment statement. Sets intent and assigns accountability.
Not a procedure — no step-by-step instructions.

### Template

---
**[ORGANISATION] — WORK HEALTH AND SAFETY POLICY**
**Document no.**: [Ref] | **Version**: [n.n] | **Effective date**: [DD/MM/YYYY]
**Review date**: [DD/MM/YYYY] | **Owner**: [Role]

---
**COMMITMENT**
[Organisation name] is committed to providing and maintaining a safe and healthy workplace
for all workers and others affected by our work. We recognise that the effective management
of work health and safety is both a legal obligation and a core business value.

**SCOPE**
This policy applies to [describe scope: all operations, specific divisions, AU/NZ, etc.],
including all employees, contractors, subcontractors, labour hire workers, and visitors.

**OBLIGATIONS**
[Organisation] will, so far as is reasonably practicable:
- Eliminate or minimise risks to the health, safety, and welfare of workers
- Consult with workers on matters that affect their health and safety
- Comply with all relevant WHS legislation and applicable codes of practice
- Provide information, instruction, training, and supervision to enable safe work
- Investigate incidents and implement corrective actions to prevent recurrence
- Continuously improve WHS performance through monitoring, review, and reporting

**ACCOUNTABILITY**
- The Board / CEO: [accountability statement]
- Managers and Supervisors: [accountability statement]
- Workers: [accountability statement]
- WHS Team: [accountability statement]

**REVIEW**
This policy will be reviewed [annually / every 2 years] or following significant changes
to the business, legislation, or following a serious incident.

**Signed**: _____________________ [CEO / MD]  **Date**: ___________

## 7. WHS Procedure Template

### Purpose
Detailed instructions for performing a specific WHS activity consistently and safely.
More detailed than policy; less task-specific than a SWMS.

### Template Structure

**Document title**: [e.g., Incident Investigation Procedure]
**Document no.**: [Ref] | **Version**: [n.n] | **Effective date**: [DD/MM/YYYY]
**Review date**: [DD/MM/YYYY] | **Owner**: [Role] | **Authorised by**: [Role]

**1. Purpose** — Why this procedure exists
**2. Scope** — What and who it applies to
**3. Definitions** — Key terms used in the procedure
**4. Responsibilities** — Who does what (table format: Role | Responsibility)
**5. Procedure** — Numbered steps; clear, instructional language; cross-references to forms
**6. Related documents** — Other policies, procedures, forms, legislation referenced
**7. Review history** — Version, date, change summary

## 8. Contractor WHS Requirements Brief

### Purpose
Communicates minimum WHS requirements to a contractor before or at contract commencement.
Not a substitute for a formal contractor management framework — supports the induction
and prequalification process.

### Template

---
**CONTRACTOR WHS REQUIREMENTS BRIEF**

**Principal**: [Organisation name]
**Contractor**: [Company name]
**Work scope**: [Brief description]
**Site**: [Location]
**Date issued**: [DD/MM/YYYY]

---
**LEGISLATIVE OBLIGATIONS**
As a PCBU, [Contractor] has primary WHS duties under [WHS Act + jurisdiction].
These duties apply to all your workers and cannot be transferred to [Principal].
Both parties have overlapping duties and must consult, cooperate, and coordinate.

**MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS — [Principal]**
Before work commences, the contractor must provide:
- [ ] Current WHS Management System or equivalent documentation
- [ ] Public liability and workers compensation insurance certificates
- [ ] Evidence of relevant licences and competencies for the scope of work
- [ ] SWMS / Job Hazard Analysis for all high-risk tasks
- [ ] Emergency response plan / contact list for the site

During work, the contractor must:
- Attend and comply with site induction
- Report all incidents, near misses, and hazards to [Principal] within [X hours]
- Maintain records of toolbox talks, inspections, and training
- Not engage sub-contractors without prior written approval
- Comply with all site rules and critical risk standards

**CRITICAL RISKS ON THIS SITE**
[List the critical risks relevant to the work scope and site — e.g., working at height,
live electrical, mobile plant, confined space, hazardous substances]

For each critical risk, the contractor must have verified controls in place before work
commences. Controls will be verified by [Principal WHS / Supervisor] before start.

**INCIDENT REPORTING**
All incidents must be reported to [Name / Role] at [contact] immediately.
Serious injuries, fatalities, and dangerous incidents must also be reported to
[relevant regulator] — both [Principal] and [Contractor] may have reporting obligations.

**NON-COMPLIANCE**
Non-compliance with these requirements may result in work stoppage, removal from site,
and termination of the contract.

**Acknowledgement**
By signing below, the contractor confirms they have read, understood, and will comply
with these requirements.
